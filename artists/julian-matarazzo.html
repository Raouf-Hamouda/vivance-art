Julián Matarazzo

Born Barranquilla, ColombiaLives and works in Barranquilla, Colombia

Julián García, known artistically as Julián Matarazzo, is a Colombian visual artist whose practice spans painting and sculpture. He began his artistic studies at the Escuela Distrital de Artes in Barranquilla and has continued developing his practice through independent study and further training, including coursework at New York Studio School.

Matarazzo’s work explores memory, identity, human relationships, and the emotional associations embedded in everyday objects. His compositions often feature elongated figures, childhood scenes, musical references, and familiar materials that function as points of connection between personal experience and collective memory.

Music occupied an important place in his early creative life and continues to inform the rhythm and emotional structure of his work. Family scenes, moments of play, and memories of growing up in Barranquilla are transformed into stylized visual narratives. Rather than reproducing the past literally, Matarazzo uses these references to reflect on the invisible bonds, exchanges, and forms of interdependence that shape human experience.

Working across canvas and sculptural materials such as marble and construction blocks, Matarazzo combines traditional processes with playful contemporary elements. The contrast between the permanence of stone, the intimacy of painting, and the modular quality of LEGO-like forms allows him to explore the relationship between fragility and structure, nostalgia and transformation, individuality and connection.

His recent series Pantone Caribe expands this investigation through the relationship between color, taste, memory, and territory. Inspired by fruits, ingredients, dishes, and sensory experiences associated with the Colombian Caribbean, the series transforms color into a language of cultural identity. Shades associated with guineo, patilla, mandarina, ciruela, guandú, and other flavors become markers of lived experience and evoke the emotional geography of the region.

Presented at Galería Barceló in Barranquilla in 2025, Pantone Caribe included paintings and sculptures and reflected on the Caribbean as a broad and interconnected cultural space. Matarazzo has also participated in solo and group exhibitions, art fairs, and auctions in Barranquilla and Bogotá. In 2025, his work was included in a collective presentation of Colombian artists in Paris.
