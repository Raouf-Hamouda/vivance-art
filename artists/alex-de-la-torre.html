Alex de la Torre

Born Barranquilla, ColombiaLives and works in Barranquilla, Colombia

Alex de la Torre Carbonell is a Colombian visual artist whose multidisciplinary practice encompasses figurative painting, sculpture, assemblage, and installation. He studied Plastic Arts at the Universidad del Atlántico in Barranquilla. His early training included working with artist Manuel Bustos and later serving as an assistant to Haime Álvarez Correa. His installation Dinero, presented at the Alliance Française in Barranquilla, received academic distinction as his laureate graduation project.

De la Torre’s practice moves between realism and conceptual construction. Through layered compositions, symbolic imagery, found and industrial objects, and carefully orchestrated visual narratives, he examines memory, social and political tensions, consumer culture, and the relationship between personal experience and collective history. Figurative painting remains central to his work, while sculpture and installation allow his images to extend into physical space.

In recent bodies of work, including Extinción and Sierra Nevada, the artist has focused on ecological fragility and the growing effects of the climate crisis. Fire, smoke, firefighters, hoses, and extinguishers appear as recurring visual signs, transforming scenes of environmental emergency into reflections on responsibility, vulnerability, and the possibility of collective action.

His solo exhibitions include Dinero at the Alliance Française in Barranquilla; Paralelos at Galería Institucional Comfamiliar; Naturaleza at Galería de la Aduana; and Testigos: Volumen II at the Consular Gallery of Colombia in New York. The latter evoked the atmosphere, cultural memory, and distinctive visual character of Barranquilla and the Colombian Caribbean.

De la Torre has participated in exhibitions and art initiatives in Colombia, the United States, Spain, and Venezuela. He received first place in the Painting–Established Artist category of the Salón Distrital de Artes in both 2017 and 2019, was preselected for Figurativas 2017 at the European Museum of Modern Art in Barcelona, and received the Young Artists Chevrolet Prize in 2008. His recent collaborative projects include Lazos y Trazos, bringing contemporary art into dialogue with the textile traditions of artisans from Chorrera, Atlántico, and Café Arte Musa, a collective tribute to Colombian writer Álvaro Cepeda Samudio.
