Rosario Heins

Born Barranquilla, ColombiaLives and works in La Grande-Motte, France

Rosario Heins is a Colombian visual artist whose painting is deeply rooted in the light, color, music, and everyday life of the Caribbean. She studied Plastic Arts and Painting at the School of Fine Arts in Barranquilla from 1975 to 1979 and later pursued studies in sculpture and printmaking at the School of Fine Arts in Cartagena. Her training in printmaking included working with Colombian artist Humberto Giangrandi.

Drawing and painting are central to Heins’s practice. Working on canvas and paper with acrylic paint, charcoal, and colored pencils, she constructs luminous scenes inspired by the beaches of the Colombian Caribbean and the Mediterranean coast. Her compositions are distinguished by saturated colors, detailed observation, rhythmic arrangements, and an intense treatment of tropical and Mediterranean light.

Street vendors, palenqueras, bathers, families, inflatable toys, fruit, sunglasses, and other beach merchandise populate her visual universe. Beneath the apparent joy and spontaneity of these scenes lies a sensitive social perspective. Heins pays tribute to informal workers and to the resourcefulness known in Colombia as el rebusque, portraying the dignity, generosity, and resilience of people who make their living along the coast.

Memory, migration, and cultural identity are also fundamental to her work. Based in southern France, in La Grande-Motte, Heins maintains a close connection with her native Colombia, returning regularly to the Caribbean as a source of artistic and emotional renewal. Her paintings bring these territories into dialogue, transforming personal memories into images that explore belonging and the coexistence of leisure and labor within the same public space.

Her solo exhibitions include presentations at Galería de la Aduana in Barranquilla, the Museum of Modern Art of Cartagena, the Palacio de la Proclamación in Cartagena, Espace Louis Feuillade in Lunel, and cultural venues in Montpellier, La Grande-Motte, Biarritz, Paris, Havana, Santiago de Chile, New York, Geneva, and other cities. In 2017, she was invited to create a monumental mural measuring approximately 38 meters for the M.U.R de Pérols in France.

Recent exhibitions include Oficio: Cartagena at the Palacio de la Proclamación in 2023–2024 and Éclat de Soleil at Galerie d’Art Prévert in Mauguio in 2026. These presentations continued her exploration of Caribbean and Mediterranean beach life, balancing an atmosphere of freedom, color, and happiness with a lucid awareness of the social and economic realities experienced by informal workers.
