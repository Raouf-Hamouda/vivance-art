Gonzalo Jiménez

Born Barranquilla, ColombiaLives and works in Barranquilla, Colombia

Gonzalo Jiménez Orozco is a Colombian photographer whose work explores territory, memory, cultural identity, and everyday life in the Colombian Caribbean. Although his first vocation was landscape design, photography gradually became his principal artistic language. His background in architecture, interior design, and landscape design continues to shape his understanding of space, proportion, light, and the relationship between people and their surroundings.

Jiménez approaches photography with a distinctly pictorial sensibility. He considers each photograph as a constructed image in which light, geometry, atmosphere, and fleeting details come together to reveal poetic dimensions of ordinary experience. His preference for agile photographic equipment allows him to respond intuitively to unexpected encounters and to record moments that might otherwise pass unnoticed.

Portraiture and landscape converge in his practice. Rather than treating the natural environment as a passive backdrop, Jiménez presents territory as a living presence shaped by history, human experience, and cultural memory. His first vocation as a landscape designer remains visible in his attention to vegetation, topography, scale, spatial rhythms, and the interaction between built and natural environments.

His photographic project Ancestral La Guajira: A Living Culture Between the Desert and the Sea focuses on the Wayuu people and the distinctive territory of La Guajira. Through portraits and landscapes, the series explores the relationship between ancestral memory, community, and an environment defined by the desert, wind, and sea. The photographs bear witness to a living culture whose knowledge, identity, and traditions have been transmitted across generations.

The project presents La Guajira not as an empty or inhospitable landscape, but as a territory rich in life, memory, and cultural meaning. Jiménez’s images invite viewers to encounter the dignity and resilience of the people who inhabit it while recognizing the profound connection between Wayuu culture and the natural world.

His exhibitions include La fotografía de Gonzalo Jiménez at Combarranquilla in 2011; La palabra, identidad, patrimonio y globalización, presented during the Festival de la Cultura Wayuu in Uribia; El otro lado de la luz at the Centro Cultural Colombo Americano in Barranquilla and the Museo Bolivariano in Santa Marta; Rostros y máscaras del Carnaval at Combarranquilla; and La mirada pictórica de Gonzalo Jiménez at the Alliance Française in Barranquilla.

In 2026, Jiménez presents Ancestral La Guajira with Vivance Art as part of the Latin America and Caribbean Weeks — SALC 2026 in France. The exhibition introduces his photographic exploration of the Wayuu people to an international audience, emphasizing the continuity of ancestral memory and the intimate relationship between cultural identity, landscape, and environmental awareness.
